Teachers Guide

Welcome Teachers!

Welcome to the Teacher’s Guide for the Service Dog project! This guide is designed to provide you with the resources and structure needed to effectively use this project with your students. Whether you are teaching about animal behavior, service dog training, or the legal and ethical considerations surrounding service dogs, this project will engage students with a hands-on approach to learning.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the role of service dogs: Students will explore how service dogs support individuals with disabilities and the training they undergo to provide this essential support.
  • Recognize legal rights: Students will learn about the rights of service dog handlers and the laws protecting those rights, including access to public spaces.
  • Explore the process of obtaining a service dog: Students will investigate the different ways people can acquire a service dog, whether through self-training, adoption, or specialized service dog programs.
  • Reflect on the role of service dogs in society: Students will analyze the impact of service dogs, considering their role in improving the quality of life for individuals with disabilities.

Suggested Activities

  • Research and Present: Have students research various types of service dogs and the specific tasks they are trained for. Students can then present their findings, sharing information on different service dog breeds and the legal framework around them.
  • Case Studies: Provide students with real-world case studies that involve service dog handlers. These can be scenarios where the dog’s role is critical, and students can discuss the ethical and social aspects of these situations.
  • Empathy Exercise: Have students participate in a role-play activity where they simulate being a person with a disability who relies on a service dog. This can help students understand the challenges faced by service dog handlers in everyday situations.
  • Discussion on Public Awareness: Lead a discussion about public misconceptions regarding service dogs. What do people often get wrong, and why is it important to educate the public on the proper etiquette and respect for service dogs?

Corgi

About the Corgi

The Corgi, famous for its adorable short legs and fox-like face, is a beloved herding dog with a big personality packed into a small frame. There are two distinct breeds: the Pembroke Welsh Corgi and the Cardigan Welsh Corgi, both originally bred for herding cattle in Wales. Known for their intelligence, playfulness, and loyalty, Corgis are both hardworking and affectionate companions.

Physical Characteristics

  • Size:
    • Males: 19-22 Inches tall, weighing between 30-45lbs.
    • Females: 18-21 Inches tall, weighing between 27-42lbs.
  • Coat: Double-coated with a medium-length, weather-resistant outer coat. Common colors include red, sable, fawn, black and tan, and blue merle (Cardigans only), often with white markings.
  • Life Expectancy: 12-15 years.

Personality and Temperment

Corgis are bold, intelligent, and affectionate dogs that pack a lot of personality into their compact size. They are excellent companions for families and individuals who enjoy an active lifestyle.

  • Energy Level: Moderate to high; they require daily exercise to stay happy and healthy.
  • Social Traits: Friendly and outgoing, they typically get along well with children and other animals.
  • Training: Highly intelligent and eager to please, making them quick learners. However, they can be a bit stubborn at times, so consistent, positive reinforcement works best.

History and Origins

The Corgi has its origins in Wales, where it was developed as a herding dog. The Pembroke Welsh Corgi is associated with Pembrokeshire, while the Cardigan Welsh Corgi hails from Cardiganshire. Both breeds share a similar history, with roots dating back over 1,000 years. Corgis were bred to herd cattle by nipping at their heels and dodging kicks, a skill aided by their low stature and agility. The Pembroke gained international fame as Queen Elizabeth II's favorite breed, while the Cardigan is known for its slightly larger frame and rounded ears.

  • Exercise Needs: Corgis are active dogs that need at least 30–60 minutes of exercise daily. Activities like walks, fetch, or herding games keep them physically and mentally stimulated.
  • Grooming:
    • Shedding: Heavy shedders, especially during seasonal changes.
    • Regular Brushing: Brush their coat several times a week to manage shedding and keep their fur healthy.
  • Health Concerns:
    • Common Issues: Hip dysplasia, intervertebral disc disease, and obesity.
    • Monitor their weight carefully, as their long backs are prone to strain.
  • Diet: Provide high-quality, age-appropriate food. Avoid overfeeding to maintain a healthy weight.

Fun Facts About the Corgi

  1. Royal Companions: The Pembroke Welsh Corgi is famously associated with Queen Elizabeth II, who has owned over 30 Corgis.
  2. Nimble Herders: Despite their short legs, Corgis are incredibly fast and agile, making them excellent herders.
  3. Distinct Breeds: Cardigans are slightly larger with rounded ears and a longer tail, while Pembrokes have pointed ears and a docked tail.
  4. Mythical Roots: Welsh folklore says Corgis were the steeds of fairies, and some markings on their coats resemble fairy saddles!


Is a Corgi Right for You?

Corgis are an excellent choice for those seeking a playful, loyal, and intelligent companion. While they’re small in stature, their energy and personality are boundless. They thrive in homes where they receive plenty of exercise, mental stimulation, and affection. Be prepared for shedding and their occasional stubborn streak, but their charm and companionship make it well worth the effort.
